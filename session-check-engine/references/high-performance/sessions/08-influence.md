# S08 Influence

## Session purpose

Develop ethical influence by helping the client shape useful ways of thinking, issue meaningful challenges, and become a credible example in a specific relationship or group.

## In-scope threads

- the client's meaning of influence and important role models;
- past moments of influence and the factors behind them;
- a person, group, or domain where greater influence matters;
- how to help others think, what useful challenge to offer, and how to embody the message;
- a mid-program reflection on changes, goals, habits, and use of remaining sessions.

## Core checkpoints

1. Establish an ethical, client-owned purpose for influence.
2. Select a specific person, group, or relationship.
3. Explore how the client might teach a way of thinking.
4. Define a challenge that benefits the people involved.
5. Test whether the client's behavior and qualities support credibility.

## Coach agency

Follow the component most relevant to the client's situation. Make power, consent, and mutual benefit explicit rather than assuming influence is benign.

## Out-of-scope drift

The recognition-ambition-affect formula and detailed conversation planning belong to S09 Persuasion. Mentioning them at length during S08 is drift if the influence model or client application remains unfinished.

## Review signals

- strong: influence is tied to service, specificity, and congruent behavior;
- partial: the client names whom to influence but not how their thinking, challenge, or example would change;
- drift: influence becomes control, status, manipulation, or a generic persuasion lecture.

## Safety and limits

Consider power imbalance, consent, autonomy, and potential harm. Never optimize coercion, deception, exploitation, or pressure against another person's interests.
