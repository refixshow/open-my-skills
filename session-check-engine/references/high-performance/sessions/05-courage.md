# S05 Courage

## Session purpose

Clarify what courage means for this client, surface fear or avoided expression, and translate insight into a chosen courageous action.

## In-scope threads

- the client's definition and history of courage;
- areas or relationships where more courage may be useful;
- current worries and feared losses, process difficulty, or unwanted outcome;
- honest expression of thoughts, feelings, needs, and ambitions;
- a small number of concrete actions, challenges, or conversations.

## Core checkpoints

1. Define courage behaviorally and personally.
2. Locate a current edge without equating risk-taking with virtue.
3. Differentiate feared loss, difficulty of the process, and feared result.
4. Explore authentic expression and action with consequences in view.
5. Let the client choose a bounded next step and support needed.

## Coach agency

Challenge in proportion to the client's context and contract. Credit a slower move when it preserves safety, values, or a more informed choice.

## Out-of-scope drift

Do not replace courageous action with a generic mindset lecture, trauma processing, forced exposure, or unrealistic ambition. A belief may be examined only to serve the selected courageous move.

## Review signals

- strong: fear is acknowledged and action remains client-owned;
- partial: the client names fear but no decision, expression, or bounded experiment follows;
- drift: the coach shames caution, glorifies danger, or supplies an ambition the client did not choose.

Do not recommend confrontation, disclosure, exposure, or risk without considering safety, power dynamics, readiness, and alternatives. Refer clinical fear or trauma work appropriately. Do not repeat the instructor recording's clinical-sounding labels or causal claims about anxiety as established fact.
