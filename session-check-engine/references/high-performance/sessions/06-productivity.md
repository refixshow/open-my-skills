# S06 Productivity

## Session purpose

Align systematic, repeatable action and results with the client's mission, priorities, projects, people, and protected time.

## In-scope threads

- a period of strong productivity and the factors behind it;
- low-productivity areas and the evidence for that judgment;
- mission, direction, and desired pace;
- distractions and competing interests;
- priority tasks, key projects and actions, necessary contacts, and dependencies;
- protected time for a meaningful project.

## Core checkpoints

1. Distinguish meaningful progress from activity volume.
2. Tie productivity to mission or a chosen result.
3. Identify the main constraints or distractions.
4. Reduce the plan to priority actions, people, dependencies, and time blocks.
5. Establish ownership and a realistic review cadence.

## Coach agency

Use only the planner components needed by the client's bottleneck. Avoid filling every field when one project or constraint deserves depth.

## Out-of-scope drift

Do not substitute a motivation or mindset lecture for prioritization. Do not turn the session into generic time-management advice disconnected from the client's mission and results.

## Review signals

- strong: the client distinguishes important progress from busyness and chooses protected action;
- partial: a large task list is produced without priority, feasibility, or dependencies;
- drift: the coach dictates a productivity system or treats more output as inherently better.

Do not frame exhaustion, disability, illness, caregiving, or unsafe workload as a discipline failure. Protect recovery and realistic constraints.
